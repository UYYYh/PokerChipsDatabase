Poker Chips Database is an application designed to record multi-table poker tournaments and cash games hosted in Imperial College London’s Eastside Linstead Hall student accommodation.

The system introduces a virtual currency called K-Kash — named after me, as both the developer and organiser of the poker nights — to let players participate casually without using real money. Each registered player starts with 20,000 K-Kash representing their virtual bankroll, and the application keeps track of all tournament and cash game results, updating every player’s balance automatically.

While initially inspired by an Elo-based ranking model, the chips system proved far more effective in practice. Its simplicity offered players greater freedom in choosing stakes and timing, and K-Kash soon became a symbol of prestige — with large balances proudly flaunted as marks of skill and status. Over time, the currency even spread beyond poker, being used for light-hearted bets on everything from table football to “whose delivery arrives first.”

This may not have been my most technically complex project, but it’s certainly the one I’m most proud of — it built a warm, welcoming community and sparked countless lasting friendships through the hall-wide poker nights I ran.
